\paragraph{What attribution errors look like.}
Under the implicit prompt, misattributed Quranic citations overwhelmingly name
the wrong chapter rather than the wrong position within it: 75.7\%
% source: summary.md, Task 1 > Implicit > Quran, wrong surah 187/247 classified
of classified cases name a different surah, against 13.4\%
% source: summary.md, Task 1 > Implicit > Quran, right surah wrong verse 33/247
that name the right surah with the wrong verse number. A further 7.3\%
% source: summary.md, Task 1 > Implicit > Quran, nonexistent/misspelled 18/247
give a surah name that resolves to no surah. Hadith citations behave the same
way: 83.2\%
% source: summary.md, Task 1 > Implicit > Hadith, wrong collection 119/143 classified
of classified misattributions name a collection that does not contain the
report, while 16.1\%
% source: summary.md, Task 1 > Implicit > Hadith, overclaim 23/143
overclaim, naming two collections or \textit{muttafaq `alayh} when only one
source carries the text. Among the collections named on misattributed Hadith,
\d{S}a\d{h}\=i\d{h} Muslim (32.9\%)
% source: summary.md, Task 2 > Implicit collections table, 92/280 mentions
and \d{S}a\d{h}\=i\d{h} al-Bukh\=ar\=i (23.9\%)
% source: summary.md, Task 2 > Implicit collections table, 67/280 mentions
are the most frequent. Isnad errors are rarer, at 91 citations,
% source: misattributions.csv, condition=implicit AND isnad_wrong=True
and concentrate on a single Companion, Ab\=u Hurayra (27).
% source: summary.md, Task 2 > Companions wrongly named (implicit), 27/66
Misattribution rates differ sharply between tiers: both frontier models stay
near zero (Gemini-3.1-Pro 0.6\%, $n=323$; GPT-5.4 1.3\%, $n=152$),
% source: summary.md, Task 3 > Implicit, Wilson CIs [0.2,2.2] and [0.4,4.7]

citations under this prompt for a stable rate.
